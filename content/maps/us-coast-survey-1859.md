US Coast Survey, “Entrance to San Francisco Bay,” US Coast Survey, 1859.

Source: [David Rumsey Map Collection](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/s/51hcbz)

In 1859, US Coast Survey mapped the entire Bay. The survey omits the interior of the San Francisco peninsula, and represents topography with hatching, rather than the more precise topographic lines. The map also shows the built environment only selectively. For example, the downtown area shows only a handful of buildings around Drumm and Davis Streets. (The cartographer’s method for selecting which buildings to show is unknown.) However, the map presents several notable developments since the 1853 survey. Most prominently, the eastern shoreline shows the extension of the wharfs more than two blocks further into the bay in places. The shoreline just south of Market Street also shows the establishment of new wharves. In the area later to be named North Beach, Meigg’s Wharf was marked as “unfinished” in 1853, but appears completed here. The 1859 survey is also notable in its representation of the Presidio. The US military had established the complex as a base in 1850, but little was done to the site for more than ten years. The map shows the settlement in idealized form, complete with the grid pattern as mandated by the 1680 additions to the Laws of the Indies. However, only four extant buildings are shown at the complex, with perhaps an additional small outbuilding on the western edge–further evidence of the decay of Spain’s original military settlement.
