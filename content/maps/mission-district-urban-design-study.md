Okamoto/Liskamm, “Mission District Urban Design Study,” San Francisco City Planning Commission, 1966.

Source: [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/missiondistrictu1966okam/mode/thumb)

In 1966, the architectural firm of Okamoto/Liskamm produced this urban renewal study for the San Francisco Redevelopment Agency (SFRA). The proposal was unique among urban renewal plans for several reasons. Unlike with previous plans in the Western Addition, Yerba Buena, and Golden Gateway areas, neighborhood-based groups had originally requested an SFRA study of the Mission, and it was those same groups that originally hired the design firm. The proposed redevelopment area comprised 423 gross acres, or 271 net acres when street area was factored out. Rehabilitation was the treatment recommended for 70 percent of the structures within the survey area. Of the remaining 30 percent, only a small fraction would be cleared; the rest required no treatment at all. The plan called for large clearances only in the areas immediately surrounding the two planned rapid transit stations, one at Sixteenth and Mission Streets, and the other at Twenty-Fourth and Mission Streets. The plan would have cleared between eight and twelve square blocks. In spite of the fact that neighborhood groups had originally supported an urban renewal plan for the Mission District, they would eventually oppose the Okamoto/Liskamm Plan.
