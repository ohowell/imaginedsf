The United States Coast Survey was the nation’s first federal scientific agency. Thomas Jefferson established the Survey in 1807, and in 1970 the Nixon administration moved the agency’s functions to the new National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA). The original purpose of the Survey was to chart the nation’s coastal waters, making them safe for navigation. Given this mandate, it is not surprising that the maps would provide the most reliable documentation of how San Francisco’s coastline changed through the nineteenth century. But they also provide perhaps the best record of how the built environment expanded and changed, and even how the topography of the city was altered to accommodate development.

Imagined San Francisco includes the surveys from 1853, 1857, 1859, 1869, 1884, and 1926. The site does not include the [1897 survey](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~205021~3002300:Magnetic-Ranges-for-San-Francisco-B?sort=Pub_List_No_InitialSort%2CPub_Date%2CPub_List_No%2CSeries_No&qvq=w4s:/where%2FSan%2BFrancisco%2B\(Calif.\);q:coast%20survey%20san%20francisco;sort:Pub_List_No_InitialSort%2CPub_Date%2CPub_List_No%2CSeries_No;lc:RUMSEY~8~1&mi=32&trs=37), which maps only “Magnetic Ranges for Compass Deviation,” nor the [1905](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~4227~360039:San-Francisco-entrance%2C-California-?sort=Pub_List_No_InitialSort%2CPub_Date%2CPub_List_No%2CSeries_No&qvq=w4s:/where%2FSan%2BFrancisco%2B\(Calif.\);q:coast%20survey%20san%20francisco;sort:Pub_List_No_InitialSort%2CPub_Date%2CPub_List_No%2CSeries_No;lc:RUMSEY~8~1&mi=34&trs=37) and [1941](https://www.davidrumsey.com/luna/servlet/detail/RUMSEY~8~1~289048~90061008:San-Francisco-Bay--U-S-C--%26-G-S--55?sort=Pub_List_No_InitialSort%2CPub_Date%2CPub_List_No%2CSeries_No&qvq=q:coast%20survey%20san%20francisco;sort:Pub_List_No_InitialSort%2CPub_Date%2CPub_List_No%2CSeries_No;lc:RUMSEY~8~1&mi=65&trs=127) surveys which focus on anchorage in the bay, and include few details of the city that do not appear in other maps.
